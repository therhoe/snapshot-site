What you have now

GA4 gives you sessions and a conversion rate per page. A heatmap gives you a pretty picture of one page on a good day. Your agency gives you a list of twenty things, all of which are defensible and none of which are ranked.

So you ship something, the number moves, and nobody can say whether it was the change or the month.

What’s actually missing

Three answers. Which page is costing you the most right now. What real people do on it before they leave. And whether the thing you shipped last month actually worked.

Every one of those is a comparison, and comparisons are exactly what date-range analytics can’t give you.

Why a snapshot, and not a date range

A snapshot measures a fixed number of real human visitors rather than a window of time. That one decision is what makes the numbers worth anything: two snapshots are directly comparable no matter how traffic moved between them. Your before-and-after becomes a fact instead of an argument about seasonality, a sale you forgot about, or a week of bot traffic.

The app does the measuring

Snapshot is live on the Shopify App Store and free to install. It answers four questions, and deliberately not a hundred.

  • 01

    Which page is costing you the most?

    Conversion rate and revenue per page, so the first thing you fix is the thing that was actually losing money.

  • 02

    Who is actually arriving?

    Every source split into humans, zombies and bots. A channel that looks strong on sessions often looks very different once they come out.

  • 03

    What are they inspecting?

    Every element that got clicked and what happened next — so a button with plenty of clicks and no purchases shows up as the distraction it is.

  • 04

    How did they leave?

    Closed the tab, went idle, clicked away, or moved on to checkout. The shape of the exit says whether the page lost them or passed them along.

The framework behind the recommendations

Numbers tell you where people stall. PACE tells you why. It came out of years of qualitative and quantitative research with retail and DTC brands, and it maps the four stages every purchase runs through — whether it takes four minutes or four months.

  1. P

    Prognosis

    What they already believe will fix their problem. Your page either confirms that theory or argues with it.

  2. A

    Activity

    Where they looked before they got here — and what that route taught them to expect.

  3. C

    Criteria

    The private checklist. Half of it is what you must have; half is what rules you out on the spot.

  4. E

    Event

    What finally tips the decision into an order. Plenty of stores lose people who had already decided.

Miss the stage a shopper is actually in, and the page answers a question they haven’t asked yet. That’s most pages.
