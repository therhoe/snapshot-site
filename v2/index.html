// 01 — the framework

PACE

Not how fast someone shops — the order they move in. Every purchase runs through the same four stages, whether it takes four minutes or four months. Miss the stage a shopper is actually in and the page answers a question they haven’t asked yet.

  1. P stage 01

    Prognosis

    their guess at what fixes the problem

    Nobody wants a product. They want progress, and they “hire” something to make it — the Jobs To Be Done idea, credit to Clayton Christensen. A shopper arrives with a theory about what will work. Your page either confirms that theory or argues with it.

    • problem → solve it
    • pain point → relieve it
    • promise → attract them with it

  2. A stage 02

    Activity

    where they go looking, and what they do there

    Depending on what they’re buying, they go hunting — or a brand comes hunting for them. Social, search, ads, a friend’s recommendation, forums, articles, reviews. By the time they reach you, that route has already shaped what they expect to find.

    • where do they go?
    • what do they do when they get there?

  3. C stage 03

    Criteria

    how they judge whether it’s right for them

    Every shopper carries a private checklist. Some of it is what the product must have; just as much of it is what would rule you out on the spot. Most pages are written entirely for the first list and completely ignore the second.

    • specs & features they need
    • disqualifiers that end it instantly

  4. E stage 04

    Event

    what finally pushes them to buy

    Wanting it isn’t buying it. Something has to tip the moment over — a deadline, a nudge, a price, a competitor who got there first. Plenty of stores lose people who had already decided, simply because nothing turned the decision into an order.

    • are they waiting on something?
    • do they need a nudge?
    • is it urgent, or can it wait forever?
    • can a competitor poach them first?

// 02 — the app

Snapshot CRO

PACE tells you what to look for. Snapshot shows you where it breaks on your store — a Shopify app that watches what real visitors actually do, then ranks the pages where a few points of lift are worth the most.

stage what snapshot shows
P Prognosis What they arrived expecting. The pages people land on, what they search for once they’re inside, and what they filter by — the gap between the store you built and the one they came looking for.
A Activity Which routes bring real people. Every traffic source split into humans, zombies, and bots. Zombies arrived and did nothing — no scroll, no click — and on most stores they’re a bigger share than anyone expects. A channel that looks strong on sessions often looks very different once they come out.
C Criteria What they inspect, and what they ignore. Every element that got clicked and what happened after — so a button with plenty of clicks and no purchases shows up as the distraction it is. Which photo is actually selling the product. What header and footer clicks say the page failed to answer.
E Event Where the push failed. The funnel from sessions down to real users, added to cart, and converted — next to how people actually left. Closed the tab, went idle, clicked away, or moved on to checkout. The shape of the exit tells you whether the page lost them or simply passed them along.

$ why it’s called a snapshot

A snapshot measures a fixed number of real visitors rather than a date range. That one decision is what makes the numbers worth anything: two snapshots are directly comparable no matter how traffic moved between them, so a before-and-after is a real before-and-after instead of a quiet argument about seasonality.

// 03 — the offer

The 1:1 Strategy Call

The app will show you what’s happening. This is where we work out what to do about it. One call, your store, the four stages — and a ranked list of what to fix first.

what we do on the call

  1. Walk your store as a shopper. Not a feature audit — we follow the route a real customer takes and mark where the page stops answering them.
  2. Place it against PACE. Which stage your store serves well, and which one it skips. Most stores are strong at Criteria and silent on Event.
  3. Read what the data already says. If Snapshot is installed we go through your real numbers together. If it isn’t, we work from what you have.
  4. Rank the work. You leave with an ordered list, not a pile of ideas — what to change first, what it should move, and how you’ll know it worked.

who it’s for

Shopify stores with enough traffic for the numbers to mean something, and someone on the other side who can actually ship a change. It is not a done-for-you service and not a sales call.
